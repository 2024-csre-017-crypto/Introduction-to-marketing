Value of Marketing

Why marketing matters to business functions and society as a whole.

Core Business & Social Value

  • Creates Demand: Finance, operations, and accounting won't matter without sufficient product demand[cite: 1].
  • Drives Financial Success: Profitability stems from sustained market demand[cite: 1].
  • Societal Impact: Increases living standards, creates employment opportunities, and expands production[cite: 1].
  • Social Needs Profitably: Marketing identifies and meets social needs profitably[cite: 1].
Real-World Example (Google): People wanted quick, reliable access to information; Google identified this social need and created Google Search[cite: 1].

If two parties are actively seeking to sell something to each other, both are considered marketers[cite: 1].

Needs, Wants & Demands

The core building blocks of customer motivation[cite: 1].

Needs

States of felt deprivation (e.g., physical food, clothing, warmth, safety)[cite: 1].

Wants

The form human needs take as they are shaped by culture and individual personality (e.g., wanting a Burger)[cite: 1].

Demands

Human wants that are backed by purchasing power[cite: 1].

Transformation Flow: Physical Hunger (Need) Craving Pizza (Want) Purchasing with Money (Demand)

Market Offerings & Marketing Myopia

Avoiding the dangerous trap of product-centric thinking[cite: 1].

Marketing Myopia (Product-Focused)

The mistake of paying more attention to the specific products a company offers than to the benefits and experiences produced[cite: 1].

Example: Focusing on manufacturing better drill bits instead of understanding the customer's true need for a hole in the wall[cite: 1].

Customer-Need-Focused Thinking

Focusing on underlying customer needs, overall satisfaction, and continuous value creation[cite: 1].

Example: Providing efficient home improvement solutions that fulfill the broader experience requirement[cite: 1].

Customer Relationship Groups

Classifying customers by potential profitability and projected loyalty[cite: 1].

Butterflies

High Profitability / Short-Term Loyalty

Good fit between company offerings and customer needs; high profit potential but temporary[cite: 1].

True Friends

High Profitability / Long-Term Loyalty

Strong fit; highest profit potential and strong loyal patronage[cite: 1].

Strangers

Low Profitability / Short-Term Loyalty

Little fit between company offerings and needs; make no investments in them[cite: 1].

Barnacles

Low Profitability / Long-Term Loyalty

Limited fit; highly loyal but low spending; requires management[cite: 1].
